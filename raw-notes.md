# TradeFlow NG — Evidence Review and Producer-to-Bulk-Buyer Discovery

Date: 16 September 2026

## Conclusion and sources

Current evidence supports investigating specific agricultural trading decisions and established workarounds. We have not established sufficient willingness to pay for standalone TradeFlow intelligence. The founder reports an early negative signal among traders reached so far. This is not proof that traders will not pay or that the trader model has failed.

Producer-to-bulk-buyer connection is a new, unvalidated hypothesis. No supplied farmer or organizational buyer case yet establishes the problem, its frequency/cost, the stronger pain, a payer, or whether matching is the needed intervention. Start with recent transaction discovery on both sides. Proceed to one bounded manual test only if the evidence warrants it. Software, forecasting and optimization are not prerequisites.

The current canvas reviewed is the founder's “Evidence-Led Lean Canvas — MVP Discovery Canvas — September 2026,” preserved in the prior project raw-notes.md. The original lean-canvass.pdf is a superseded draft. This assessment also uses recorded Ibadan interview findings, founder-relayed trader accounts, and the new context supplied with this request. No new interviews, transaction verification or external market research occurred for this review.

Earlier session notes are preserved in raw-notes-before-producer-buyer-review-2026-09-16.md. This file is the current assessment. Product requirements and locked scope have not changed.

## Classification rules

- Observed evidence: past/current behaviour directly observed, recorded or reported by a real customer. A customer report is evidence of their account, not independent proof of causation, financial amounts or representativeness. Provenance is stated below.
- Working hypothesis: a current explanation, customer definition, service or experiment that has not been adequately tested. Proposed actions, measures and scope choices belong here; writing them down does not validate them.
- Future assumption: a later capability, advantage, expansion or revenue opportunity without sufficient present evidence.

Every working hypothesis and future assumption below is explicitly unvalidated. Founder plans and operational resources are distinguished from customer evidence. Proposed experiment sizes are research choices, not invented customer facts. Compound canvas claims are split where their evidence status differs.

## 1. Evidence we currently have

- E1: Recorded Apata interview findings describe restocking triggered by low stock or customer demand, price-confirmation calls, direct visits and drivers. Retailers and a rice wholesaler appear. This establishes existing workflows, not a uniform information problem.
- E2: One recorded demo response says a recommendation would not change behaviour because the respondent sells within the same market. A trial checkbox on the same interview does not override that objection. This is a negative signal for that proposition, not every possible service.
- E3: The founder reports roughly ten onboarded local sellers and trusted suppliers. This weakens recruited-customer fit for the original cross-market routing product. It does not prove that cross-market traders do not exist or that trust makes switching impossible.
- E4: Rashsidat's account, relayed by the founder: previous market-day sales/demand guide her quantity; eight baskets bought, seven sold, one later discounted. She wanted current prices and an idea of demand. No purchase/sale amounts, basket weights or exact date were supplied. Discounting does not establish net loss or causation by missing information.
- E5: The founder identified Saturday morning as Rashsidat's quantity-change window. Exact commitment time is unresolved. Dikko's mainly Tuesday/Saturday routine is founder-reported and does not apply automatically to other markets.
- E6: Latest founder context reports an early negative signal for standalone intelligence and strong influence from relationships, current methods and profit decisions. Sufficient willingness to pay has not been established. This is not a claim that all traders reject payment.
- E7: Accessible trader contacts, field personnel and technology are reported operational resources. They are not farmer/buyer customer evidence. No completed new-model intervention is documented.

Do not assume the anonymous Niger State seller, Rashsidat and every registered trader have identical circumstances. Do not combine separate accounts into a stronger invented case.

## 2. Evidence we do not have

- A named producer's recent costly or failed sale, with crop/grade, quantity/unit, availability window, counterpart and consequence.
- A named organizational buyer's recent costly or failed purchase, role, specification, deadline, approval authority and consequence.
- Recurrence across ordinary transactions, including successful cases and acceptable workarounds.
- Evidence separating a matching failure from price, quality, quantity, credit, delivery, payment risk or another constraint.
- Actual available supply and an authorized purchase need compatible on product, place, timing and terms. An interest list is neither confirmed supply nor confirmed demand.
- Actual farmer/buyer objections to TradeFlow, rather than hypothetical objections.
- Quantified workaround costs, attributable service benefit or reliable delivery cost.
- Identified payer, payment authority, accepted paid terms, paid trial or repeat payment for the new model.
- Evidence that software, a marketplace, forecasts or optimization would help either side.
- A defensible comparison of which side has stronger, more frequent pain.
- Detailed records behind the intelligence-model negative signal: offer, quoted price if any, customer choice, refusal reason, timing, actual payments and subsequent behaviour.

Decision: keep these unknown. Do not substitute generic sector claims, market averages or old canvas numbers for customer evidence.

- H1: A narrow producer or buyer segment experiences repeated consequential transaction failures inadequately handled by existing relationships.
- H2: A bounded check, introduction or coordination task can address an identified failure before the decision deadline.
- H3: Actual supply and demand can align on specification, amount, location, time and payment without untested TradeFlow financing or delivery obligations.
- H4: At least one identifiable beneficiary with authority receives enough incremental value to pay.
- H5: Actual accepted payment could sustain the service's repeat delivery cost.

All five are unvalidated working hypotheses. Historical data advantages, forecast-assisted matching, optimization, marketplace software and geographic expansion remain future assumptions.

## 5. Most important hypotheses requiring immediate validation

1. Pain before matching: establish one actual consequential incident, then investigate recurrence; do not assume a general complaint is frequent pain.
2. Mechanism before product: identify the failed step and whether TradeFlow can affect it before the deadline. A directory cannot solve every failure.
3. Compatibility before introductions: verify a real authorized need and an actual available lot against all material requirements; the same crop name is insufficient.
4. Behaviour before expansion: record concrete next steps such as supplying a real specification, permitting a relevant introduction or evaluating a lot, plus refusals and abandoned steps.
5. Payer before commercial claims: identify who benefits, who approves spending and an explicit service offer. Interest, conditional payment acceptance and actual payment are different evidence levels.

## 6. Provisional problem statement

Some interviewed agricultural traders make buying and selling decisions through established relationships and past experience; at least one reports later discounting unsold tomato stock. The evidence does not yet establish the causal information gap or sufficient demand for paid standalone intelligence. For the new model, we are investigating whether a specific producer or organizational purchaser encounters a repeated failure in completing an acceptable produce transaction that existing methods cannot adequately resolve. The failure, its timing and consequence must be established before calling it a matching problem.

This is a discovery statement. No producer/buyer pain can yet be stated as an observed fact.

Choose the buyer category through access to actual transaction accounts, not by assuming hotels, processors, restaurants and institutions have interchangeable needs.

Tomatoes are a proposed discovery starting crop because the team has trader context; this does not establish farmer access or buyer demand. ## 8. Provisional value proposition

For a producer or produce-purchasing organization with a demonstrated transaction obstacle, test whether one manual verification, introduction or coordination step helps complete an acceptable transaction more reliably than the current method. Select only the intervention supported by evidence. Do not promise available stock, buyers, quality, delivery, payment, lower prices or higher profit before demonstrating the ability to provide it.

Plain-language repeat-back: “We are investigating where a real produce sale or purchase breaks down, and whether a small manual service can fix that step well enough for someone to pay.”

A sharper customer-facing promise is not yet defensible. Inventing specificity would hide the discovery gap.

## 9. Smallest real-world experiment before software

### Stage A — Recent transaction discovery

Proposed starting batch: three actual producers and three actual purchasing decision-makers in one buyer category, discussing the same crop and feasible geography. This is a practical discovery sample, not a representative survey or a claimed validation threshold. Existing traders may provide introductions; their opinions are not substitutes for interviews with the proposed participants.

1. Ask about the most recent transaction, then a recent difficult one. Record date, participant role, counterparty, product/grade, quantity/unit, required timing, payment terms and who decided. Do not begin by pitching matching.
2. Reconstruct exactly where the process worked or failed, the workaround and the consequences. Separate recollection, estimates, supporting records and unknowns. Ask how often the issue occurred across transactions they can actually recall or document; preserve that denominator.
3. Ask about the last rejected offer, supplier or order and why. Discover relationship benefits and switching costs without suggesting intermediaries are wasteful.
4. Identify the next real sale/purchase and its deadline. Establish who can commit stock, authorize a purchase, approve a new supplier and approve payment. Those may be different people.
5. Write one incident record per participant: job, timeline, failure point, workaround, consequence, recurrence, next transaction, requirements and actual objections. Include participants whose current method works well.

If the batch reveals no repeated consequential failure, pause this model for that segment. If only a subgroup has the problem, narrow the investigation and retain the sample limitations. Do not proceed because people politely agreed to talk.

### Stage B — One conditional manual intervention

Proceed only when Stage A identifies a real unresolved problem and a participant actively wants help with an upcoming transaction.

- Record their original plan and usual fallback before intervention.
- Define one bounded task, such as checking a lot against an actual specification or introducing a suitable counterparty. These are options, not a required bundle. If the actual problem is financing or transport beyond current capability, stop and reconsider the proposed service.
- Check the participants' authority, actual stock/need, units, grade, quantity, timing, location and payment/collection expectations. Preserve incompatibilities rather than manufacturing a match.
- Obtain permission from both parties before sharing private contacts or making an introduction. This experiment does not require TradeFlow to buy goods, hold funds, book transport or guarantee delivery/quality.
- Record what actually happens: specification shared, lot checked, introduction accepted/refused, terms discussed, order accepted, transaction completed or abandoned. An introduction is not an order; an order is not an accepted and paid delivery.
- If the parties independently transact, follow up on acceptance, timing, payment status, complaints and results where evidence is willingly shared. Distinguish TradeFlow's contribution from the existing fallback. One success does not establish causation or repeatability.
- Record all observer/operator time, airtime, transport and follow-up expenses, including unsuccessful attempts. Unpaid work is still delivery effort.
- Once service scope and economic beneficiary are clear, discuss an explicit service offer with the actual budget owner. Set any test price using discovered value, alternatives and measured delivery cost, not an invented number in this document. Record stated interest, conditional agreement and actual agreed payment separately. A free trial does not validate payment willingness.

Calls and a simple incident/transaction log are sufficient. No app, marketplace, forecast or optimizer is needed. This document does not send messages, conduct interviews or execute transactions.

## 10. Criteria to support, weaken or reject the new hypothesis

These are prospective decision rules, not achieved results. Rejecting a narrowly tested proposition is not rejecting the entire agricultural market.

| Hypothesis | Evidence supporting further testing | Evidence weakening it | Evidence to reject or pause the tested version |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A repeated consequential problem exists. | Independent participants reconstruct similar actual failures, consequences and recurrence grounded in their transactions. | General complaints, isolated inconvenience, vague recall or an acceptable existing workaround. | Incident reviews consistently find no consequential recurring failure in the selected segment. Pause that segment. |
| Matching or verification addresses the failure. | A missing counterparty or unresolved uncertainty blocks a transaction, and the bounded intervention resolves that step before its deadline. | Parties already know counterparts; assistance duplicates current calls; the mechanism remains uncertain. | The failure consistently involves price/credit, quality or logistics the proposed service cannot change. Reject matching alone for that case. |
| Compatible actual supply and demand exist. | Authorized buyer requirements and an available lot align on essential terms, with concrete next steps by both parties. | Gaps in timing, grade, unit, volume, location or payment; only hypothetical future interest. | The real terms cannot be reconciled without unacceptable cost or obligations. Do not call it a viable match. |
| Parties will accept TradeFlow's involvement. | Permission to verify/share information, a relevant introduction and actual follow-through. | Polite interest, hesitation, withheld essential details or dependence on existing partners. | Repeated explicit refusal of the defined intervention with no acceptable adaptation in the tested segment. Preserve the reasons; silence alone is not conclusive market rejection. |
| A party has economic reason and authority to pay. | Identified budget owner bears an avoidable cost and accepts explicit service terms; actual agreed payment is stronger evidence. | Praise, free participation, no budget authority or benefits accruing to someone else. | No identified beneficiary authorizes a sustainably deliverable priced offer after testing appropriate decision-makers. Reject that payer/offer combination, not every possible model. |
| Service can be delivered economically and repeatedly. | Measured costs are compatible with actual accepted payment, responsibilities are manageable and a participant brings another real transaction. | Exceptional founder effort, omitted costs or a one-off relationship explain success. | Repeat attempts require costs/obligations beyond acceptable payment with no credible narrower service. Pause scaling. |
| Software or existing models are needed next. | Repeated manual success exposes a specific task where a tool could be tested to reduce effort/errors without changing the promise. | Manual delivery is unstable, volume unknown or automation addresses no observed bottleneck. | Reject software as the next step while problem, intervention and payer remain unsupported; it may be useful later. |

### Interpreting the first batch

One documented case supports investigation of that case. Independent recurrence strengthens the problem hypothesis. One manual intervention tests feasibility, not market size or repeatability. A paid service and voluntary repeat request provide stronger evidence, but still require cost and outcome review. Investigate contradictory cases as carefully as successes. Do not use signup totals or expressions of enthusiasm as substitutes.

### Immediate action

Use existing contacts to recruit an actual producer and an actual organizational purchasing decision-maker for recent-transaction interviews, then complete the small batch within one selected commodity and buyer category. Do not pitch a marketplace during these calls. Return with the incident records, including examples where current methods work well. Choose the smallest intervention only after a concrete failure emerges.

Possible results include support for information, procurement assistance, a verified introduction, another service or no viable intervention by TradeFlow. The purpose of this experiment is to distinguish these outcomes, not to make the farmer-to-bulk-buyer story succeed.
